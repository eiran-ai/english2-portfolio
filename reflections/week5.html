Week 5 Reflection

Session 1: Persuasive Essay - Outline and Introduction

We moved from expository writing to persuasive writing this week. The big difference is that in a persuasive essay, you're not just explaining something. You're trying to convince the reader to agree with your position. That changes everything about how you write. The hook needs to be stronger because you want the reader emotionally invested from the start. The thesis has to clearly state your position, not just preview topics. We practiced writing persuasive hooks using questions, bold statements, and scenarios. I tried a bold statement for my essay about whether Rwanda should invest more in public transportation, and my classmates said it grabbed their attention right away. It felt different from informative writing because I actually cared about convincing people, not just informing them.

Session 2: Persuasive Essay - Body Paragraphs and Conclusion

This session added something new that we hadn't done before: counterarguments and rebuttals. In a persuasive essay, you don't just present your side. You also acknowledge what the other side might say and then explain why your position is still stronger. That was hard at first because it felt weird to argue against myself. But my instructor explained that addressing counterarguments actually makes your essay more convincing because it shows you've thought about the issue from all angles. We also learned about the call to action in the conclusion. Instead of just summarizing, you tell the reader what they should do or think differently about. I liked that part because it gives the essay a purpose beyond just getting a grade.

Ezechiel Irankunda - Kepler College SNHU26 - English Communications 2
