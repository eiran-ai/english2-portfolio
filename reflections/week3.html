Week 3 Reflection

Session 1: Informative Essay - Body Paragraphs and Conclusion

This session completed the informative essay. We focused on body paragraphs and conclusions. Each body paragraph needs a topic sentence, supporting details, evidence, analysis, and a concluding sentence. The part I struggled with was analysis. I'd present a fact or a quote and then just move to the next point. My instructor compared it to giving someone ingredients without a recipe. The evidence is the ingredient, but the analysis is where you explain what to do with it. I started asking myself "so what?" after every piece of evidence, and that helped a lot. For the conclusion, we learned to restate the thesis in different words, summarize the main points, and leave the reader with a final thought. It's not just copy-pasting the introduction at the end.

Session 2: Compare and Contrast Essay

This session introduced compare and contrast essays. We learned two ways to organize them: block style and chain style. Block means you write everything about subject A first, then everything about subject B. Chain means you go point by point, switching between A and B in each paragraph. I tried block first in my practice essay but it felt disconnected. The reader had to remember all the details about A while reading about B. When I switched to chain style, the essay flowed better because each paragraph handled one point of comparison directly. We also practiced using transition words for comparison like "similarly" and "likewise" and for contrast like "on the other hand" and "in contrast." Those small words make a big difference in how smooth the writing feels.

Ezechiel Irankunda - Kepler College SNHU26 - English Communications 2
