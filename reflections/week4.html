Week 4 Reflection

Session 1: Problem-Solution Essay

This session was about problem-solution essays. The structure is pretty straightforward: describe a problem, explain why it matters, then propose solutions backed by evidence. You can do it in block style (all problems first, then all solutions) or chain style (each problem paired with its solution). What was hard for me was making sure my solutions weren't just opinions. It's easy to write "the government should do X" but that means nothing without proof that X actually works somewhere. I improved by looking up real examples from other countries and using those as evidence. For instance, when I wrote about youth unemployment, I found data about vocational training programs in Kenya that reduced unemployment in specific regions. That felt way stronger than just saying "training would help."

Session 2: Cause and Effect Essay

The cause and effect essay was interesting because it made me think about how things connect. You pick a topic and explain what caused it and what resulted from it. The tricky part was separating causes from effects when they overlap. For example, when I was writing about urbanization in Kigali, stress on infrastructure is an effect of too many people moving in, but it can also be a cause of people moving to other neighborhoods. I had to be very specific about the direction. My instructor told us to draw arrows between ideas to check if the logic flows one way. That helped me stay organized. We also talked about chain of causation, where one effect becomes the cause of something else. That concept opened up a whole new way of thinking for me. It's like dominoes.

Ezechiel Irankunda - Kepler College SNHU26 - English Communications 2
