Week 8 Reflection

Session 1: Impromptu Speech Presentation

This was the most nerve-wracking session so far. We had to do impromptu speeches. That means you get a topic and you have to start talking about it with no preparation time. I got the topic "Should universities require students to do community service?" I had about thirty seconds to think before I had to start speaking. My mind went blank for the first few seconds, but then I just started with what I knew. I talked about how community service at Kepler helps us connect with people outside the campus. It wasn't perfect and I repeated myself a few times, but I made it through. The lesson I took away is that your brain already has ideas. The hard part isn't having something to say. It's trusting yourself to say it without a script. A few of my classmates did really well. They just talked like they were having a conversation, and it sounded natural.

Session 2: Informative Speech

After the chaos of impromptu speaking, the informative speech felt more comfortable because we had time to prepare. The goal was to present information on a topic in a clear, organized way using visual aids. I chose to talk about how mobile money works in Rwanda because I know the topic well. I made a few slides in Canva with statistics and a simple diagram showing how a MoMo transaction flows from person to agent to network. What I learned is that presenting is very different from writing. In an essay, you can use long sentences and complex ideas. In a speech, you need shorter points and you have to look at your audience instead of reading off slides. My instructor told me I was looking at my screen too much and I needed to face the class more. That's something I need to keep working on.

Ezechiel Irankunda - Kepler College SNHU26 - English Communications 2
