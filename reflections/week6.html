Week 6 Reflection

Session 1: Persuasive Techniques and Canons of Rhetoric

This session was about the classical tools of persuasion. We learned about ethos, pathos, and logos. Ethos is about credibility. If the audience trusts you, they're more likely to believe what you say. Pathos is emotional appeal. You connect with people's feelings to make your argument hit harder. Logos is logic and evidence. Facts, statistics, and reasoning. I realized I'd been relying almost entirely on logos in my essays and ignoring ethos and pathos completely. We also covered the five canons of rhetoric: invention, arrangement, style, memory, and delivery. These are ancient Greek concepts but they still apply today. The one that stuck with me was arrangement because it connects directly to how we structure essays. Where you put your strongest argument matters just as much as what the argument is.

Session 2: Audience Analysis

Audience analysis was something I never really thought about before this session. We learned that before you write or speak, you need to understand who you're talking to. What do they already know? What do they care about? What might make them resist your message? We talked about social, cultural, and psychological context. For example, if I'm writing about mobile money for a class at Kepler, I can assume my audience knows what MoMo is. But if I'm writing the same topic for an international audience, I'd need to explain the basics first. We did an exercise where we had to rewrite the same argument for two different audiences, and it was surprising how much the language and examples had to change even though the main point stayed the same. That exercise made me realize that good writing isn't just about what you say. It's about knowing who's listening.

Ezechiel Irankunda - Kepler College SNHU26 - English Communications 2
