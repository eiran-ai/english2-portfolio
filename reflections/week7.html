Week 7 Reflection

Session 1: Presentation Tools

This session was a shift from writing to presenting. We explored different tools: PowerPoint, Powtoon, Canva, Prezi, Camtasia, and Nearpod. I already knew PowerPoint from Computer Applications, but the other tools were new to me. Canva stood out because it's free and the templates look really professional without much effort. Prezi was interesting too because it zooms in and out instead of going slide by slide, which makes presentations feel more dynamic. Powtoon is for animated videos, which could be useful for explaining something complex. We had to choose one tool and make a short presentation with it. I picked Canva because I liked how fast I could make something that looked good. The main takeaway was that PowerPoint isn't the only option, and sometimes a different tool fits the situation better.

Session 2: Active Listening, Ellipsis, Reductions, and Contractions

This session combined two things: listening skills and speech patterns. For active listening, we learned techniques like making eye contact, nodding, paraphrasing what the speaker said, and asking clarifying questions. We practiced in pairs. My partner would say something and I had to summarize it back to them before responding. It felt awkward at first, but I noticed that when someone actually listens and repeats what you said, you feel respected. The second part was about ellipsis, reductions, and contractions in spoken English. Ellipsis is when you leave out words that are understood from context. Reductions are when spoken words get shortened, like "gonna" for "going to." We listened to clips of native speakers and tried to identify where these patterns happened. It made me realize that spoken English is very different from written English, and both are valid. You just need to know which one to use where.

Ezechiel Irankunda - Kepler College SNHU26 - English Communications 2
