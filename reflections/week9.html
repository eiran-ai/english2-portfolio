Week 9 Reflection

Session 1: Persuasive Speech

The persuasive speech was the hardest presentation of the term. Unlike the informative speech where you just share information, here you have to actually change people's minds. I spoke about why Rwanda should invest more in public libraries. I used ethos by mentioning that I study at a place where access to resources makes a real difference. I used pathos by describing a student who couldn't afford textbooks and had to share with three classmates. And I used logos by citing statistics about how library access correlates with higher test scores. Putting all three together in a speech felt powerful. The feedback I got was that my opening was strong but I rushed through the conclusion. I think I was running out of time and got nervous. Next time I need to practice with a timer so I know exactly how long each section takes.

Session 2: Group Discussion Preparation

The last session was about preparing for group discussions. We learned about assigning roles like facilitator, note-taker, timekeeper, and presenter. We also talked about how to keep a discussion productive. That means asking open-ended questions, building on what others say, and politely disagreeing when you see things differently. We did a practice discussion about whether social media helps or hurts education. I was the facilitator for my group, which meant I had to make sure everyone got a chance to speak and that we stayed on topic. The hardest part was stopping people from talking over each other without being rude about it. I learned that being a good facilitator isn't about controlling the conversation. It's about creating space for everyone to contribute. That's a skill I know I'll use way beyond this English class.

Ezechiel Irankunda - Kepler College SNHU26 - English Communications 2
