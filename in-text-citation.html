In-text Citation

APA In-text Citations

This week I learned what APA citation is and why we need it in academic writing. We covered the two main in-text citation styles: parenthetical and narrative. A parenthetical citation puts the author and year in brackets at the end of a sentence, while a narrative citation weaves the author's name into the sentence itself. We also discussed what plagiarism is and why it matters so much at Kepler. This was important because every essay we write from now on needs proper citations, and not citing correctly can count as academic dishonesty even if you did not mean to cheat. The main challenge I faced was remembering when to include page numbers in my citations. I kept forgetting that direct quotes need page numbers but paraphrases do not always need them. I dealt with this by making a small reference card on my laptop that lists the rules side by side so I can check quickly while writing.

APA Reference List

In this session I learned how to write a full reference list at the end of a paper using APA format. We practiced citing different types of sources like books, journal articles, websites, YouTube videos, and even ChatGPT. The format changes depending on the source type, so each one has its own rules for what comes first, how titles are written, and where the URL goes. This matters because a reference list gives the reader everything they need to find your sources and check your claims. It is also what separates your ideas from borrowed ones. The hardest part was citing journal articles because there are so many parts: author, year, article title, journal name, volume, issue, and page numbers. I got confused about which parts to italicize. I solved this by using the APA citation guide as a template and filling in each field one at a time instead of trying to remember the whole format from memory.
