Informative Essay

Outlining and Writing an Informative Essay Introduction

In this session we started working on expository essays, specifically informative essays. I learned how to create an outline before writing and how to build a strong introduction that has three parts: a hook, background information, and a thesis statement. The hook grabs the reader's attention, the background gives context, and the thesis tells the reader what the essay will cover. We also looked at sample essays to see how professionals structure their introductions. This was important because I used to just start writing without planning, and my essays would go in random directions. Having an outline first keeps the whole essay on track. The challenge I faced was writing a good hook. My first attempts were boring sentences like "This essay is about..." which is not really a hook at all. I improved by studying different hook types like questions, statistics, and short stories, and I picked a surprising statistic for my essay which worked much better.

Writing Body Paragraphs and Conclusions

This session completed our work on informative essays. We focused on writing body paragraphs with strong topic sentences, supporting details, evidence, analysis, and concluding sentences. We also learned how to write conclusions that restate the thesis, summarize the main points, and leave the reader with a final thought. We analyzed a sample essay about ChatGPT and academic integrity, which helped me see how each paragraph connects to the thesis. This matters because body paragraphs carry all the evidence and reasoning that support your argument. A weak body paragraph means your reader has no reason to agree with you. The main challenge was writing analysis after presenting evidence. I kept stopping at the evidence itself and not explaining why it matters. My instructor pointed out that evidence without analysis is like giving someone ingredients without a recipe. I started asking myself "so what?" after every piece of evidence, and that helped me add the explanation the reader needs.
