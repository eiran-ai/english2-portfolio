Informative Essay: Outline and Introduction

SESSION 2: Outlining and Writing an Informative Essay Introduction

In this session we started working on expository essays, specifically informative essays. I learned how to create an outline before writing and how to build a strong introduction that has three parts: a hook, background information, and a thesis statement. The hook grabs the reader's attention, the background gives context, and the thesis tells the reader what the essay will cover. We also looked at sample essays to see how professionals structure their introductions. This was important because I used to just start writing without planning, and my essays would go in random directions. Having an outline first keeps the whole essay on track. The challenge I faced was writing a good hook. My first attempts were boring sentences like "This essay is about..." which is not really a hook at all. I improved by studying different hook types like questions, statistics, and short stories, and I picked a surprising statistic for my essay which worked much better.
