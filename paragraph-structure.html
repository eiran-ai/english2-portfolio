CER Paragraph: The Impact of Public Libraries on Student Performance

Public libraries play an important role in helping students perform better in school, especially in communities where families cannot afford to buy textbooks and study materials. A study by Krashen (2004) found that students who had access to well-stocked libraries scored higher on reading tests than students who did not, regardless of their family's income level. In Rwanda, the Kigali Public Library has become a popular study space for university students because it offers free internet, quiet reading rooms, and access to academic journals that most students cannot pay for on their own. This matters because education is supposed to give everyone a fair chance, but students who cannot access books and resources start at a disadvantage. Libraries help close that gap by giving free access to the same materials that students in wealthier families already have at home.

CLEAR Paragraph: Why Group Work Improves Learning in College

Working in groups helps college students learn more deeply than studying alone. This connects to a broader conversation about how universities can prepare students for workplaces where teamwork is the standard, not the exception. Johnson and Johnson (2009) conducted a review of over 100 studies and found that cooperative learning leads to higher academic achievement, better problem-solving skills, and stronger relationships between students compared to individual learning. At Kepler College, most of our assignments require group collaboration. For example, in my statistics class, we had to clean a dataset together in Excel, and each person was responsible for one section of the analysis. I learned more from explaining my part to my teammates than I did from reading the textbook. The reason group work is effective is that it forces you to put your understanding into words. When you have to explain something to another person, you quickly find out if you actually understand it or if you just memorized it. That process of explaining and debating builds the kind of deep understanding that sticks. Group learning pushes students beyond surface-level memorization and builds both academic knowledge and communication skills that matter after graduation.

References

Johnson, D. W., & Johnson, R. T. (2009). An educational psychology success story: Social interdependence theory and cooperative learning. Educational Researcher, 38(5), 365-379.

Krashen, S. D. (2004). The power of reading: Insights from the research (2nd ed.). Libraries Unlimited.
